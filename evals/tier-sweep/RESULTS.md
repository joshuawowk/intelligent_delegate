## What it shows

**1. Quality is at or near the ceiling in every cell.** The lowest overall score is 0.988
(`haiku/low`). On bounded work with a checkable answer, every current model at every
effort mostly gets it right. This sweep therefore ranks cells by **time and money**, and
says little about which is *smarter* — see "Saturation" below before leaning on that.

**2. Effort is the largest speed lever, larger than model.** Against the same model at
`medium`, `xhigh` took 2.9x as long on Haiku, 3.7x on Sonnet and 3.9x on Opus. Sonnet and Opus scored the same at both.
`high` took 1.4x, 1.5x and 1.9x.

**3. `low` saves almost nothing over `medium` and is where the misses are.** `low` was
17%, 8% and 9% faster than `medium` (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus). Every miss by Sonnet or
Opus in the whole sweep happened at `low`. From `medium` up, both were perfect on all
four tasks.

**4. Sonnet is the fast model. Haiku is the cheap one.** At `medium`, Haiku took 1.6x as
long as Sonnet and cost 9.5x less. Haiku is slower because it writes more: 5007 output tokens
a run against Sonnet's 1602, 3.1x as many. That is also why its measured cost advantage is
about 9.5x where its price sheet says 20x.

**5. Opus bought nothing here.** At `medium` it took 1.6x as long as Sonnet and cost 2.4x as
much for the same score. None of these tasks is the kind Opus is for.

**6. An untiered subagent in this session is the slowest cell on the grid.** A subagent
that names no tier inherits the session's model and effort — here `opus/xhigh`. Against
`sonnet/medium` that is **6.2x the time and 6.6x the cost** for the same score.

## Every miss

- **t1, t4:** none — all 60 runs of each scored 1.000.
- **t2:** `haiku/low` q9 x2; `haiku/medium` q9 x1. Every one is the same question — the message on the line *after* a named
  entry. The run's own search printed both lines and it reported the wrong one. No other
  model missed any t2 question.
- **t3:** `opus/low` B11 x1; `haiku/low` B9 x1; `sonnet/low` B9 x1; `haiku/high` B5 x1.
  - B5 (medium) — price_total truncates the discount instead of rounding half up.
  - B9 (hard) — bool values are accepted as quantities (True counts as 1), though the spec says bool does not count as a positive integer.
  - B11 (hard) — history returns the internal event dicts rather than copies, so a caller can mutate recorded history.
  Of 657 findings across the 60 review runs, 1 earned no credit and 0 were duplicates.
  The uncredited one, `t3-haiku-high-1`: "The discount uses floor division `subtotal * discount_pct // 100` rather than..." — repro fails on a correct ledger. Read per the audit rule: it is a seeded
  defect described correctly with a repro that asserts the wrong value, not a flaw in the
  fixture. No reviewer padded its report.

## The pre-registered rules, applied

**D1 — `mechanical` gets `effort: low`.** `haiku/low` on t1: mean 1.000, min 1.000, against
a bar of 0.98 and 0.95. The tier declared no effort and so ran at the session's:
`haiku/xhigh` took 31.8 s and $0.0065 on t1 where `haiku/low` took 12.9 s and $0.0037 — **2.5x
faster** at the same score.

**D2 — a read-only Haiku tier is added, at `low`.** On t2 every Sonnet arm scored 1.000; the
cheapest of them is `sonnet/low` at $0.0321 and 8.6 s. `haiku/low` scored 0.960 (min 0.900) —
within 0.05, min above 0.80 — at $0.0027, **11.9x cheaper**. It qualifies on cost, not on speed:
at 10.7 s it is slower than that Sonnet arm. The rule was written expecting Haiku to be
the fast option. It is the cheap one, and the tier is described that way.

**D3 — `inspector` moves from `high` to `medium`. Saturated.** On t3 `sonnet/medium` and
`sonnet/high` both scored 1.000 and both found every hard defect in every rep; `medium`
took 16.6 s against 27.5 s, **39% faster**, at $0.0502 against $0.0712. The rule is met, and it is
met on a **saturated** task: this shows `medium` is enough for a bounded check of an
artifact against its source, and shows nothing about review harder than that.
`haiku/medium` also scored 1.000 on t3. Recorded as a candidate; it changes nothing.

**D4 — `worker` and `drafter` are unchanged.** `worker` (`sonnet/medium`): `sonnet/low` is
not 20% faster on either task (t1 9.4 s against 8.9 s; t4 16.3 s against 18.5 s).
`drafter` (`sonnet/high`): `sonnet/medium` matches its score on both tasks and is 41% faster
on t4 but only 14% faster on t1. The rule asked for 20% on both, so `drafter` stays at
`high` and `medium` is a **candidate**. The rule's wording left open whether 20% had to
hold on each task or overall; it is read the stricter way, since that is the direction
every rule in `DECISIONS.md` leans.

**D5 — `reviewer` and `builder` are unchanged**, as the rule said they would be whatever
was measured. For the record, `opus/high` against `opus/medium`: t3 52.4 s against 29.0 s,
t4 76.1 s against 33.6 s, same scores. That is the price of an escalation on work that did
not need one, which is the reason to escalate only on a predicate.

**D6 — `judge`** was not measured and stays `opus/xhigh`.

## Saturation

47 of the 48 task x arm cells scored 0.98 or better. That is the main limit on what this
sweep can support. It is strong evidence about **time and cost**, where the cells differ
by multiples and the runs agree closely. It is weak evidence about **quality**, where
almost nothing separates them: it shows the cheaper cells are sufficient for bounded,
checkable work, and it cannot show where they stop being sufficient. The tier defaults
that moved (D1, D2, D3) moved on those terms. The escalation predicates did not move at
all, and nothing here is a reason to loosen them.
